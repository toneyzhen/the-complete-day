Where nutrients actually come from

Targets are the US Dietary Reference Intakes for your selected sex and age — the RDA where one exists, the Adequate Intake otherwise. EPA+DHA has no DRI, so it is shown for information and never counted as a gap. Manganese’s 11 mg upper limit is shown but not enforced: whole grains, nuts and legumes routinely exceed it and dietary manganese has never been shown to harm healthy people. Food values are USDA FoodData Central figures for the stated preparation, rounded; cooking losses, soil and brand variation move real numbers by more than a few percent, so treat 100% as a soft floor rather than a finish line. Iodine in seaweed varies enormously — the nori figure is the Japanese food tables’ value for toasted sheets, and published analyses range several-fold either side of it. Kelp is far higher and genuinely risky. Where a food's trace nutrient is not known with confidence it is recorded as zero, not guessed — so the app under-credits a food rather than over-crediting it, and a plan that clears every target has margin in hand. Diet patterns here are food-selection rules, not certifications: definitions of paleo and Whole30 vary by source, “raw” means the food is edible as listed without cooking, and none of this speaks to how a food was produced or prepared. Some patterns cannot clear every target by construction — carnivore has no route to fiber or vitamin C, and no unfortified plant food contains vitamin B12 — and the app will tell you so rather than pretend otherwise. Vitamin K counts K1 and K2 together. The AI is written in K1 (phylloquinone) terms because that is what intake surveys measured, but both forms are cofactors for the same enzyme, so menaquinones from natto, cheese and egg yolk count toward the same target. Sodium is a ceiling, not a target, so it is never counted as a gap: the line turns amber above 2300 mg, the level the 2019 DRI review names for reducing chronic-disease risk, and the AI is 1500 mg. It is also the one nutrient where blanks would have been the unsafe direction, so unlike every other trace in this database it is filled in for all __NFOODS__ foods from typical figures rather than left at zero. Salt added at the table is not counted unless you add it — the iodized-salt entry is there for that. Fortified foods (the fortified plant milks and juices, iodized salt, enriched bread and pasta) are marked as such and use the Nutrition Facts panel of one widely sold US product each, converted to per-100 g; brands differ, so check your own carton. Nutrients a label doesn’t print are taken from the unfortified version or left at zero. To plan from unfortified food only, pick Fortified foods under “Also avoid” in Auto-solve. This is an arithmetic tool, not medical advice.
